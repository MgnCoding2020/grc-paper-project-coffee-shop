## 1. Purpose
Every employee has their own account, and personal passwords are never shared (P-001 §5.5). But some credentials can't belong to one person: the firewall's admin login, the Wi-Fi passphrases, the internet provider's account, and the portals for the cameras and the espresso machine. Until now these have lived with Vertex IT Support, the outsourced IT provider, with no record of who knows them.

This procedure puts those shared credentials in a company password manager, limits who can see them, and rotates them whenever someone who knew them leaves. It implements NIST SP 800-53 **IA-5** (authenticator management) and supports PCI DSS **8.3** and **8.6** (loose mapping; see the crosswalk).

## 3. What Goes In, and What Doesn't

| In the password manager | Never in the password manager |
|---|---|
| Router, firewall and access-point admin logins | Any employee's personal password or MFA codes |
| Staff and guest Wi-Fi passphrases | Card numbers or any cardholder data (P-004) |
| Internet provider account | Break-glass account credentials: sealed and kept by the Owner, per Microsoft's emergency-access guidance |
| Camera system, espresso machine and sensor vendor portals | Anything for a system that supports individual accounts: those get individual accounts instead |
| Vendor support account numbers and PINs | |

## 4. Rules
  1. **Individual accounts first.** A shared credential is only stored when the system can't give each person their own login.
  2. **Generated passwords.** Every stored password is generated by the password manager: at least 16 random characters where the device allows it, and never a default password.
  3. **Rotate when someone leaves.** When a member, vendor or employee who knew a credential leaves, rotate it the same day. The staff Wi-Fi passphrase is also rotated whenever any employee leaves (P-005).
  4. **Rotate after suspected compromise**, and rotate network admin passwords once a year.
  5. **Two members, always.** If one member leaves, the Owner removes them and the business keeps access through the remaining member. The Owner adds the replacement IT Technician only after their screening and Privileged User Agreement are complete.
  6. **Check monthly.** The IT Technician confirms the organization still has only its two members (PR-008 §3).

## 5. Free Plan Limits and Compensating Controls

| Limit | Compensating control |
|---|---|
| No event log of who viewed or changed an item (Teams plan only) | Only two people have access; each item keeps its own password history; rotation on every staff or vendor change; monthly membership check |
| Only two collections | Grouped by purpose (§2); not split by person, because both members need everything in an emergency |

Moving to the Teams plan would add event logs. That's a cost decision for the Owner, recorded as an option, not a requirement.
